# 🧟 BrainRot RPG — App Store Optimization (ASO) Guide

> *A practical, no-fluff guide to getting BrainRot RPG discovered on Google Play.*

---

## Why ASO Matters for This App

BrainRot RPG is Android-only by design — which means Google Play is your *only* distribution channel. There's no iOS fallback, no web version. Every download comes from the store. Getting ASO right before launch is not optional; it's the entire top-of-funnel.

The good news: the concept is genuinely differentiated. Most screen time apps guilt the user. BrainRot RPG leans in. That's a real angle — and angles make ASO easier.

---

## 1. App Title

The title carries the most keyword weight of any field in Google Play. It's also the first thing a human reads.

**Recommended title:**
```
BrainRot RPG: Screen Time Idle Game
```

**Why this works:**
- **BrainRot RPG** — your brand name, memorable, self-explanatory
- **Screen Time** — the #1 search term users would type when looking for this kind of app
- **Idle Game** — a recognized genre tag that attracts the right audience (people who understand passive/background games)

**Character limit:** 30 characters max on Google Play. The above is 37 — trim if needed:
```
BrainRot RPG: Screen Time Game    ← 30 chars exactly
```

**Avoid:** Generic terms like "tracker" or "monitor" — those attract users looking for parental control apps, not games.

---

## 2. Short Description (80 characters)

This appears directly under your title in search results, before the user taps in. It needs to do two things: convert the curious, and include a keyword.

**Recommended:**
```
Your phone habits shape your avatar. The brainrot is the point. 🧟
```

**Alternative (more keyword-forward):**
```
Idle RPG that turns your screen time into a game character. No guilt.
```

**What to avoid:** Describing how it works technically. Users don't care about WorkManager. They care about whether this sounds fun.

---

## 3. Full Description

Google Play indexes the text in your full description for search. Keywords need to appear naturally — not stuffed — ideally in the first 167 characters (what's visible before "Read more"). Write for humans first, then sprinkle keywords.

**Recommended structure:**

---

**Turn your screen time into an RPG.**

BrainRot RPG is a passive idle game that watches what you actually do on your phone — and turns it into a character.

Every app you use earns XP. Every hour you spend shapes what your avatar looks like. Sink 40 hours into TikTok and Reels? Your character becomes the Sigma Zombie — glazed eyes, phone glued to hand, trending fits. Spend your time on Spotify and audiobooks? You evolve into the Fake Intellectual — headphones, turtleneck, suspiciously good taste.

No quests. No timers. No habits to log. Just use your phone normally and watch your avatar become a portrait of your media diet.

**Avatar Classes:**
- 🧟 **Sigma Zombie** — dominated by short-form video and social media
- 😐 **Extremely Online** — deep in YouTube, Reddit, and Twitter/X
- 🎧 **Fake Intellectual** — primarily podcasts, Spotify, and audiobooks
- 🎭 **Hybrid** — a chaotic blend of everything

**Room Objects & XP Boosts**
Spend your accumulated hours on room objects — a Pizza Tower, Bookshelf, Second Monitor, and more. Place them in your character's room and activate them for XP multipliers.

**Lifecycle System**
Each life ends at 160 hours of total screen time. Your outcome — Graduate, Die, or Ascend — depends on what you spent those hours on. Past lives are saved in your Archive forever.

**How it works:**
BrainRot RPG reads your phone usage in the background using Android's built-in screen time API. Data never leaves your device. No account needed. No ads. No judgment.

*Android only. Requires usage access permission from Settings.*

---

**Keywords woven in:** idle game, screen time, RPG, avatar, phone usage, passive game, background tracker, Android game

## 4. Keywords to Target

Google Play does not have a keyword field like the Apple App Store — keywords are pulled directly from your title, short description, and full description. Prioritize these:

| Priority | Keyword | Why |
|----------|---------|-----|
| 🔴 Must-have | `screen time game` | High intent, low competition for game variant |
| 🔴 Must-have | `idle RPG` | Genre-correct, passionate audience |
| 🔴 Must-have | `brainrot` | Trending term, your brand |
| 🟡 Important | `phone habits` | Captures screen time adjacent searches |
| 🟡 Important | `avatar game` | Describes the core mechanic |
| 🟡 Important | `passive game` | No-active-play segment |
| 🟢 Nice-to-have | `digital wellbeing game` | Counter-positioning angle |
| 🟢 Nice-to-have | `offline idle game` | Discoverability in idle game category |

**Research tip:** Type each of these into Google Play's search bar and watch the autocomplete suggestions — those are real searches from real users. Add any relevant completions to your description.

## 5. Screenshots (Your Most Important Asset)

For a game, screenshots decide whether someone taps "Install" or bounces. You have 30 seconds of visual attention. Make each screen earn it.

**Recommended 6-screenshot sequence:**

| # | What to Show | Overlay Text |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Room scene + avatar at a dramatic visual state (Sigma Zombie) | *"Your screen time. Your avatar."* |
| 2 | Side-by-side of two avatar classes (Zombie vs Fake Intellectual) | *"What are you, really?"* |
| 3 | Avatar screen with XP bar filling up | *"Every hour counts. No effort required."* |
| 4 | Shop sheet open with room objects | *"Spend your hours. Upgrade your room."* |
| 5 | Lifecycle end screen (the 💀 Die outcome) | *"160 hours. One outcome. Start again."* |
| 6 | Archive screen showing two completed lives | *"Every life remembered."* |

**Screenshot rules:**
- Use a real device or Pixel 6 emulator at 1080×2400 for crisp captures
- Add a solid dark background behind each screenshot (your dark theme already helps)
- Keep text overlays large, short, and on a contrasting band
- Do **not** show the permission screen — it makes the app look complicated

**Feature graphic (1024×500px):**
Show the Sigma Zombie and Fake Intellectual side by side against a dark background with the tagline: *"The more you scroll, the more you become."*

Your icon appears at 48×48px in search results. It needs to read instantly.

**What works for this app:**

- High contrast — dark background, bright subject
- No text (unreadable at small sizes)

## 8. Ratings & Reviews — Your #1 Post-Launch Priority

The Play Store algorithm weights review velocity and star rating heavily. A 4.6-star app with 200 reviews will outrank a better-optimized listing with 3.8 stars.

**Where to add the rating prompt:**
The existing lifecycle system is perfect for this. The moment the player sees the end screen (Graduate, Die, or Ascend) is a high-emotion moment — they just completed 160 hours. That's the right time to ask.

Add a rating dialog in `LifecycleEndScreen.kt` using the in-app review API:

```kotlin
// Add to LifecycleEndScreen — fire after the outcome is shown
val reviewManager = ReviewManagerFactory.create(context)
val request = reviewManager.requestReviewFlow()
request.addOnCompleteListener { task ->
    if (task.isSuccessful) {
        reviewManager.launchReviewFlow(activity, task.result)
    }
}
```

**Dependency to add:**
```kotlin
implementation("com.google.android.play:review-ktx:2.0.1")
```

**Do not ask for a rating:** on first launch, after a crash, mid-session, or before the user has meaningfully engaged. The end of a lifecycle is ideal — they've invested 160 hours, the outcome just landed. That's when they're most likely to rate positively.

## 9. The Restricted Permission Problem (Read This Carefully)

`PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS` is a restricted permission. Google Play manually reviews apps that use it, and they will reject the app if the justification isn't clear. This is your biggest ASO/publishing risk.

**What to submit in the Permissions Declaration:**

> *"BrainRot RPG is an idle RPG where the player's real phone usage determines their avatar's appearance, XP, and lifecycle outcome. The PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS permission is used exclusively to read per-app foreground time via UsageStatsManager. This data is processed locally on the device, never transmitted externally, not shared with third parties, and not used for advertising or tracking. It is the core mechanic of the game — without it, the app cannot function at all. Users are shown a clear permission explanation screen before being directed to Settings > Special App Access > Usage Access."*

**Make your permission flow as frictionless as possible:**
The existing `PermissionScreen.kt` is a good start, but consider adding:
- A visual showing exactly which Settings screen they need to find
- A "Check again" button that re-polls without requiring a restart
- Text that emphasizes data privacy: *"Stays on your device. Never shared."*

## 10. Localization (Post-Launch)

Once you have initial traction, localization is the highest-ROI growth lever for Android games. Google Play dramatically boosts visibility in markets where your listing is localized.

**Priority markets for this concept:**
1. **United States** — launch market, largest RPG/idle game audience
2. **United Kingdom** — strong "chronically online" culture, same language
3. **Germany** — large Android market share, appetite for quirky indie games
4. **Brazil** — massive Android adoption, high TikTok/Instagram usage = natural Brainrot tier users

The `RoomScene.kt` and all UI strings are already in `strings.xml`, which means localization is straightforward — just translate the XML file. AppTweak's localization intelligence tool can help identify which keywords perform in each market.
